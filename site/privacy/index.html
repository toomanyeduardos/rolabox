Rolabox privacy policy

Effective 21 November 2026

Rolabox is a music and podcast player for Android. It is made by Eduardo Flores, an individual developer, who is responsible for the data described here. If you have a question about this policy, or want to ask for something below, email rolabox.app@gmail.com.

Rolabox has no servers of its own, shows no ads, and doesn't sell your data or share it for advertising. Your music never leaves your phone. This page lists everything that does leave it, where it goes and why.

What stays on your phone

Rolabox asks for permission to read your audio files (READ_MEDIA_AUDIO). It reads your music files and their tags (title, artist, album, genre, track number, length) to build a library on your phone. Your music files, their tags and the library are never uploaded. Neither are your play queue, playback positions, downloaded episodes or downloaded covers. Everything about your library stays on your phone.

Rolabox doesn't ask for your location, contacts, camera, microphone or any other personal data.

Using Rolabox without an account

You can use Rolabox without an account. Then nothing is sent to Firebase Authentication or Firestore, and your settings, follows and episode orders exist only on your phone. These still apply: crash reports, album covers, podcasts and Android backup.

With an account

An account is optional. It lets you sync your settings and podcasts between devices.

What's stored

  • Your email address. If you sign up with a password, Firebase stores the password as a hash. Rolabox never sees or stores it.
  • Your Google account's name and profile photo link, if you sign in with Google. Firebase copies these from Google when you sign in. Rolabox doesn't use them.
  • An account ID, created by Firebase, that ties your synced data to you.
  • Your settings: theme, accent color, click sound, shuffle, repeat, and which rows the main menu shows.
  • The podcasts you follow, each with when you followed or unfollowed it.
  • The episode order you chose for a podcast, newest or oldest first, each with when you chose it.

This data is stored by Google Firebase (Authentication and Cloud Firestore), which may store it on servers outside your country. Security rules let only your own account read or write your synced data. It is used only to sign you in and to sync, and isn't shared with anyone else.

Crash reports

Rolabox uses Google Firebase Crashlytics to report crashes, freezes ("app not responding") and a few named errors that mean something in the app is broken. This happens for every user, with or without an account, and there's no setting to turn it off, because a report holds nothing about you or your music.

A report holds:

  • where in Rolabox's code the error happened (the stack trace) and the name of the error;
  • your device's model, Android version, free memory and storage, screen orientation, and whether it's rooted;
  • whether Rolabox was on screen, and Rolabox's version;
  • random installation IDs that Crashlytics and Firebase create for this install of the app.

A report never holds your account ID, email, the names of your songs, albums, artists or podcasts, file paths, or the text of any error message. Rolabox doesn't use Google Analytics or any other analytics. Crashlytics keeps reports for 90 days.

Album covers

When an album has no cover on your phone, Rolabox searches MusicBrainz for it and downloads the cover from the Cover Art Archive, which is run by MetaBrainz and the Internet Archive. What's sent is the album's title and artist name, and nothing about you or your account. Like any web request, these services see your phone's IP address. You can turn this off with Settings → Online Artwork.

Podcasts

  • Search. When you search for a podcast, the text you type and your phone's country are sent to Apple's iTunes Search API, which finds the shows and episodes. Nothing about you or your account is sent.
  • Feeds, images and audio. Each show's episodes, artwork and audio come from the server its publisher uses, which can be any company. Those servers see your phone's IP address and what it asks for, as with any download, under their own privacy policies. Rolabox always tries HTTPS first. A show whose server only offers plain HTTP is still fetched over HTTP, which isn't encrypted.

Android backup

If Android backup is on for your phone, Android saves Rolabox's settings and podcasts data (follows, episode orders, episodes and positions) to your own Google account's backup, and copies them when you set up a new phone. Nothing else of Rolabox's is backed up. Android and Google control this, and you can turn it off in your phone's settings.

How long data is kept

  • Your account and synced data: until you delete your account.
  • Crash reports: 90 days.
  • Data on your phone: until you delete it, clear Rolabox's storage, or uninstall the app.

Deleting your data

You can delete your account and its synced data in the app, or by email. How to delete your Rolabox account.

To see a copy of your account's data, or to correct it, email rolabox.app@gmail.com from the account's email address.

Children

Rolabox isn't directed at children under 13, and doesn't knowingly collect personal data from them. If you think a child has created an account, email us and it will be deleted.

Security

Everything Rolabox sends to Firebase, Apple, MusicBrainz and the Cover Art Archive goes over HTTPS. The only plain HTTP is to podcast servers that don't offer HTTPS, as described above.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes too. Every version is kept in Rolabox's public source code.
